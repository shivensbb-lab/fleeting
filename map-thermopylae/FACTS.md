# Facts — Thermopylae
| Claim | Source / basis |
|---|---|
| Battle in 480 BC; Persian (Achaemenid) army marching south into Greece | Herodotus, *Histories* 7.175–7.233; standard dating |
| Persia the largest empire of its time | Achaemenid Empire — conventional historiography (e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Achaemenian dynasty") |
| "Thermopylae" = "the Hot Gates" (hot springs) | Herodotus 7.176 |
| At its narrowest, room for a single cart/wagon | Herodotus 7.176 ("only a single wagon-road") |
| About 7,000 Greeks including 300 Spartans | Herodotus 7.202 lists the contingents (~5,200 Peloponnesians plus Thespians, Thebans, Phocians, Locrians); ~7,000 is the common modern total |
| Held for two days; on the third the Persians came round by a mountain path shown by a local man (Ephialtes) | Herodotus 7.210–7.218 |
| Anopaea path over the mountain, descending behind the Greeks near Alpeni | Herodotus 7.216; drawn as a schematic route |
| The coastline has moved: silt from the Spercheios and other streams | Kraft, Rapp, Szemler, Tziavos & Kase, "The Pass at Thermopylae, Greece", *Journal of Field Archaeology* 14(2), 1987, pp. 181–198 |
| ≈ 4 km between the 480 BC shore (taken here as the mountain foot at the pass) and today's shore | Measured in this film's terrain/imagery data along 22.536° E: mountain foot (20 m) at 38.797° N, water today at 38.833° N → 0.036° ≈ 4.0 km. The 480 BC shoreline is schematic |
Ancient shoreline, river course, Persian route and Anopaea path are schematic and labelled so on screen.
