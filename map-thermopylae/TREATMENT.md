# Thermopylae: The Pass That Moved — treatment

Style: 3D Map Explainer. Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~50 s, English narration (Kokoro bm_lewis), burned-in legend-shelf subtitles.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Funnel** — globe → Greece → the pass → back out. The style's default; the globe opening is the demo's cliché. Rejected.
2. **Before/after on the same terrain** — show the pass as it was (sea at the foot of the cliffs), then the line that beat it (the mountain path), then the sea pulling back to today's coastline. *Chosen*: the story is literally about the ground changing, and the map is real ground.
3. **Journey along one line** — the Persian march from the Hellespont. Too long for 50 s; the drama is at one point.

## Logline and arc
In 480 BC, 7,000 Greeks held a gap between mountain and sea a cart wide; a goat path behind the cliffs undid them — and today, the sea itself has gone.
Setup: the narrow pass. Turn: the Anopaea path ignites behind the mountain. Ending: the old shoreline drawn, then today's coast kilometres away — the pass no longer exists.

## Benchmark
Learn from news 3D terrain explainers (low raking light, dive and level-out) and Kings and Generals battle maps (one movement at a time). Don't take their colours, icons, unit markers or music.
