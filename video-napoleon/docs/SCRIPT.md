# NAPOLEON WASN'T SHORT
### Under 7 Minutes — Short — shooting script v1

10 shots x 5s = 0:50 of picture, vertical 9:16. Narration ~100 words, shown as on-screen captions (no voiceover).
Refrain: "Nobody checked."

## COLD OPEN

**A01_1** — A tiny Napoleon stickman in a bicorne hat stands on a wooden box to reach a map table inside an 1800s French army tent.
> Everyone knows Napoleon was tiny. Everyone is wrong.

**A01_2** — Napoleon steps off the box and is exactly as tall as the soldier stickman beside him.
> About five foot seven. Average for a Frenchman, maybe taller.

**A02_1** — Two rulers side by side, one French and one British; no people, the rulers are plain inanimate objects.

**A02_2** — A French inch block grows visibly longer than a British inch block; no people, inanimate objects.

**A05_2** — Napoleon at normal size folds his arms, deadpan.

## Deliberate omissions and why

- **Exact Imperial Guard height minimums** — sources give different figures for different regiments and years; "picked for height" is the safe claim.
- **"Napoleon complex" as a named condition** — the term is twentieth-century and contested as psychology; no retrospective diagnosis.
- **Exact centimetre heights** — kept to "about five foot seven" because the conversion depends on which autopsy record and which inch.

## Sources (for the description)

- F. Antommarchi's autopsy record (1821), height given as 5 pieds 2 pouces in French units.
- The French pouce: about 2.707 cm; the British inch: 2.54 cm.
- James Gillray's caricatures of "Little Boney" (British Museum collection).
- "Le petit caporal": nickname from his troops after Lodi, 1796.
