# The Great Emu War — treatment

Under 7 Minutes, episode. 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 fps, about 6:30. English narration (Kokoro, British male), burned-in captions and `.srt`.
Style: the user's own **Under 7 Minutes** style (draft in `style/`), made from a blank page with techniques from
Mid-century Cartoon (`midcentury-toon`) in Lemo-Opuscar. The script and every line of narration are in `lines.json`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The campaign diary.** Day-by-day log of the operation, each day a "dispatch" card. Clear, but it starts slow and
   the comedy depends on dates the audience doesn't care about.
2. **Myth first, then the escalation ladder** (chosen). Cold open on the absurd image (soldiers vs emus), kill the
   myth in the first 30 seconds ("it wasn't a war"), then climb: why the farmers were there → why the emus came →
   round one → the ambush and the truck → retreat → round two → the commander's surrender-by-compliment → verdict.
   Each rung ends on a bigger problem, and the refrain "and the emus were fine" lands on every rung's punchline.
3. **The emu's point of view.** One emu narrates the whole thing as a nature documentary. Funny, but it puts invented
   speech into the centre of a history film and loses the channel's deadpan narrator.

Chosen: **2**. It is the channel's format (myth-bust in the first minute, Causality → Action → Punchline per act,
one refrain), it has a natural acceleration (the ambush and the truck chase) and a real breath (the withdrawal), and
its ending can echo the opening image exactly.

## 2. Logline and arc

An army that won a world war takes two machine guns to a wheat field and loses, politely, to a bird.

- **Setup**: soldier-settlers on bad land, broke after the Depression; 20,000 emus arrive.
- **Turn**: the army comes; the emus split up, the gun jams, a bird crashes the truck; the army withdraws.
- **Ending**: round two "works" at ten bullets a bird; the commander praises his enemy; bounties and fences do what guns
  couldn't. Final image: the same lone emu on the same hill as the opening, unbothered. "The emus were fine."

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from** the deadpan animated-history explainer (the genre the channel belongs to) and 1950s UPA classroom
  shorts: one sentence per picture, flat colour-plane rooms, held bodies with moving hands and eyes, numbers that land on
  the picture, a narrator who underreacts.
- **Don't take**: any existing channel's characters, character designs, title card, music or catchphrases. Our cast,
  emu design, cards and refrain are our own.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 100 bpm in 4/4 for the body, 132 bpm for the ambush, half-time 66 bpm for the retreat. Shots cut on the
nearest beat after the line ends plus 0.6 s. Instrumentation: plucked strings and woodblock for the farmers,
a small marching snare and tuba-like low pluck for the operation, the snare doubling at the ambush, music out
on the withdrawal (the silence), a single sustained pad under Meredith's quote, the full theme back for the verdict,
a button on "fine".

## 8. Captions and titles

Captions are part of the style: bold white sans with a heavy black outline, bottom centre above the safe line, the
accent word in yellow, one caption per line of narration, held for the whole line plus 0.6 s. The title card is a
heavy outlined slab of white capitals. Numbers are digits on screen and words in the voice.
