# MEDIEVAL PEOPLE KNEW THE EARTH WAS ROUND
### Under 7 Minutes — Short — shooting script v1

20 shots x 2.5s = 0:50, vertical 9:16, hard cuts, loops. Narration as on-screen captions (no voiceover).
Refrain: "The Earth: still round."

Shot list follows src/data/shots.json, one narration line per 2.5 s shot:
cold open (warn, twist, title); the Greeks (greek, stick, close); the Middle Ages (monks, sphere, orb, round1);
the real argument (size, small, food, right, america); the inventor (irving, drama, books, round2); loop.

## Deliberate omissions and why
- **Exact accuracy of Eratosthenes** — depends on which stadion he used; worded as contested on screen.
- **Lactantius and Cosmas Indicopleustes** — two genuine flat-Earth writers, but marginal and not representative; too nuanced for 50 seconds.
- **The Salamanca commission details** — accounts of the 1486–87 deliberations are thin; kept to "the experts said he'd run out of food" (the distance objection).

## Sources (for the description)
- Cleomedes, *On the Circular Motions of the Celestial Bodies* (account of Eratosthenes' measurement).
- Johannes de Sacrobosco, *De sphaera mundi* (c. 1230), standard university astronomy text.
- Washington Irving, *A History of the Life and Voyages of Christopher Columbus* (1828).
- Jeffrey Burton Russell, *Inventing the Flat Earth* (1991).
